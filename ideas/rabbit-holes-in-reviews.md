# Rabbit holes in reviews

Rabbit holes are topics I've said I want to dig into later, like the ones in ~/rabbitholes/. The app could keep them too. Every now and then a review session would surface one I parked, the way it surfaces a due card, so they don't rot in a folder. This needs care, because a rabbit hole isn't a Card: there's nothing to recall. It's an invitation.

## Why folders fail: Pull vs. Push

Things rot in `~/rabbitholes/` because folders require *pull* (you have to remember to open them), whereas spaced repetition has *push* (a daily habit loop with scarcity and pacing).

## The Zeigarnik vs. Closure trap

Mixing rabbit holes directly into the middle of a review queue creates a psychological conflict:

- **A review session is a convergence loop**: closing open loops, driving the due count to zero, high tempo (10–15 seconds per card).
- **A rabbit hole is a divergence loop**: opening a loop, 20 minutes of open-ended exploration.

If a rabbit hole appears on card #4 of 20, the user has two bad choices: ignore it to finish their reviews (training themselves to swipe past rabbit holes just like browser tabs), or take the bait and abandon the remaining 16 due cards.

## Design principles

1. **Place the invitation at the threshold of completion.** Surface a rabbit hole as the "dessert" after the due queue hits zero (or at a natural batch boundary), when the user is already in a curious, warmed-up mental state and has earned closure on their obligations.
2. **Give a rabbit hole only three honest responses:**
   - **Sparked** → Start a conversation / make a first Card.
   - **Still curious** → Resurface in $N$ weeks.
   - **Let it go** → Compost it (explicitly celebrating letting go of old curiosity without guilt).
